Prevention of Bullying

For School & Educators For Parents & Families For Students
Create a safe environment: Establish a school culture where bullying is not accepted and promote a sense of inclusion and respect. Communicate openly: Check in with your child frequently, listen to their concerns without judgment, and let them know you are a trusted resource. Be a good friend: Show kindness to those who are being bullied. Stand with them, talk to them, and help them feel included.
Educate students: Teach students what bullying is, how to stand up to it safely, and the importance of reporting it to an adult. Model positive behavior: Show kindness and respect to others and handle conflicts calmly at home. Speak up: If you are being bullied, tell a trusted adult, like a parent, teacher, or counselor, immediately.
Empower students and staff: Empower students to report bullying and provide them with a safe space to talk about it. Support the school: Familiarize yourself with the school's anti-bullying policy. If you see bullying: Do not just watch; report it to an adult.
Increase supervision: Monitor areas where bullying is more likely to occur, such as playgrounds, bathrooms, and hallways, and have all staff members actively watch for and respond to bullying. Build confidence: Encourage your child's interests and hobbies, as this boosts self-esteem and helps them make friends. Be mindful online: If you receive messages or see things online that make you sad or scared, talk to an adult.
Develop social skills: Implement programs that help students build resilience, communication, and empathy. Monitor online and offline activities: Get to know your child's friends and set clear rules for online behavior. Report bullying you see: If you see someone else being bullied, report it to an adult so they can help..
Address bullying incidents seriously: Step in quickly to help children who are being bullied and work with them and their parents to develop a solution. Partner with the school: Establish a relationship with teachers and staff, attend school events, and work with them to create an anti-bullying plan if needed. Know how to respond: If you can, stand up to the bully by saying "stop" in a firm, confident voice. Avoid using anger or tears
Stay safe: If the direct approach doesn't work, walk away. Stay near other kids or adults and avoid being alone in places where bullying might happen.

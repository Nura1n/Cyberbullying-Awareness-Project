Types of Bullying

Physical Bullying

>> is saying or writing mean things.

Hitting/kicking/pinching

Spitting

Tripping/pushing

Taking or breaking someone’s things

Making mean or rude hand gestures

Verbal Bullying

>> involves hurting a person’s body or possessions.

Teasing or insulting a person

Name-calling

Inappropriate sexual comments

Taunting

Threatening to cause harm

Unfairly blaming or criticizing someone

Humiliating or ridiculing a person

Manipulating someone

Social Bullying

>> sometimes referred to as relational bullying, involves hurting someone’s reputation or relationships.

Leaving someone out on purpose

Telling other children not to be friends with someone

Spreading rumors about someone

Embarrassing someone in public

Cyberbullying

>> occurs over digital devices, including computers, cell phones, and tablets.

Disseminating harmful or humiliating content about someone

Sending threatening or unwanted messages or content to a person

Sharing a person’s private information

Spreading rumors through digital means

Prejudicial Bullying

>> involves online or in-person bullying based on the target's race, ethnicity, religion, or sexual orientation.p>

>> it is based on stereotypes and is often a result of the belief that some people deserve to be treated with less respect than others.

>> Though prejudicial bullying has been studied less than other types of bullying indicates that ethnic and sexual minorities are more likely to be bullied than their peers.

Sexual Bullying

>> involves physical, verbal, or online bullying that includes unwanted sexual actions or behaviors.

Touching a person without their consent

Making sexual jokes

Making lewd gestures

“Sexting,” which involves sharing or sending sexual messages, images, or videos

Spreading sexual rumors
